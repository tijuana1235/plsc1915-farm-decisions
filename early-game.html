Read the comparison

Capital, cash, and return

Capital committed

Tool fees plus the sale value of materials used for new equipment. Both plans face the same capital budget. Buying seeds is an operating cost.

Cash for seeds

Cash fees reduce the gold available for planting. Owned materials still have an opportunity cost even though crafting does not remove gold from the wallet.

Net return

Sales received minus seed purchases, tool fees, and new material value. Starting gold is not revenue. Unsold crops, products still in machines, and pending payments are shown separately.

ROI against keeping equipment

Return on investment compares each plan's added net return with the capital it commits. A zero-capital plan has no defined ROI.

Plan A versus Plan B

Subtract B's net return from A's. To assess extra spending, incremental ROI divides the higher-capital plan's added net return over the cheaper plan by its extra capital. Equal capital gives no defined incremental ROI.

Recovery and time

Recovery is the first day a plan catches up with keeping equipment and stays ahead through the cutoff. Time savings are reported in game hours, without assigning a wage. A useful choice can depend on goals beyond crop receipts.

ROI vs. keep = (plan net return − keep-equipment net return) ÷ plan capital × 100

Compare net return, cash needs, and workload as well as the percentage. These are conditional classroom calculations with no discounting or residual equipment value.

Inspect the model

Equations, game rules, and classroom assumptions

The game determines crop growth, sale prices, equipment coverage, and upgrade costs. A published game formula does not establish an exact farm work rate. Our walking and action timings are fixed classroom assumptions, so capacity and payback are conditional estimates.
